# Five fixes make homemesh feel native

Modern iOS UI in late 2026 means one thing above all: **Liquid Glass is reserved for a floating "functional layer" of system bars, sheets, menus and a few key controls, while content stays on solid, semantic-colour surfaces underneath** ([Apple: Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass); [HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). iOS 27 refined that material rather than replacing it, and the compatibility opt-out stops working for iOS 27 SDK builds ([Apple: UIDesignRequiresCompatibility](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/uidesignrequirescompatibility)). Most of the modern look therefore comes free when you build with the current SDK and delete custom chrome. homemesh already builds with Xcode 26.6, so its bars and sheets are already glass. What makes it feel like an engineering tool is everything else. It has **84 `.font(.footnote)` calls, 52 `status = "…"` strings as its only feedback, no app icon or accent colour, zero animations, zero haptics, and an 8-segment tool picker** on its main work surface. The five highest-value changes, in order, are these. First, rebuild the plan screen's tools, inspector and zoom. Second, replace footnote status with a real feedback layer: banners, toasts, haptics, `ContentUnavailableView` and labelled progress. Third, turn `HouseView` into a hub led by SF Symbols, and replace alert-based creation with small sheets. Fourth, give the apps a brand and a tone system taken from scshafe-ui's tokens. Fifth, discipline the glass on the scan overlay and on parley's bars. Changes two, four and five apply to parley, mailroom-ios and jobtrack-ios as well, and they are exactly what a first **scshafe-ios** release should package. That release would be one Swift package with an iOS 17 floor. Its tokens would be generated from a *fresh* `@scshafe/ui` 0.9.0 snapshot by a port of scshafe-qt's `gen_tokens.py`. Two older sources must not be used: the owner's local scshafe-ui checkout (stale at 0.5.0) and scshafe-qt's snapshot (0.4.1). Every Liquid Glass call would sit behind one `#available(iOS 26, *)` shim. The repository would be public so that macOS GitHub Actions CI runs, and releases would use SemVer tags.

## iOS 26 rewards deleting chrome more than adding glass

Apple's own adoption checklist is blunt about where the work lies. "If your app uses standard components from SwiftUI, UIKit, or AppKit, your interface picks up the latest look and feel," and "standard components like bars, sheets, popovers, and controls automatically adopt this material" ([Apple: Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)). Bordered buttons become capsules by default. Partial-height sheets become inset glass. Confirmation dialogs morph out of the buttons that present them. Toolbar items float on glass and get a scroll edge effect "applied to content under system toolbars" ([WWDC25 session 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). Lists and forms get taller rows, larger section radii and section headers that are no longer all capitals ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)). The flip side is that custom backgrounds now fight the system: "If your app has any extra backgrounds or darkening effects behind the bar items, make sure to remove them" ([WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). The HIG goes further: "Instead of applying a solid or semi-opaque background color beneath controls, use a scroll edge effect" ([HIG: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)).

The prohibitions matter as much as the APIs. **"Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer"**, use it "sparingly", never put glass on glass, and only use the `.clear` variant over media-rich backgrounds with "a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity" ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials); [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Too many glass effects outside a `GlassEffectContainer` "can degrade performance" ([Apple: Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views)). Colour should be applied sparingly and should carry meaning: "When every element is tinted, nothing stands out... If you want to imbue color into your app, do it in the content layer instead" ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). And colour must never be the only signal ([HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). The rest of the HIG canon is unchanged but now enforced by the system's look. It asks for system text styles with Dynamic Type up to at least 200% ([HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)) and for 44×44 pt targets. It wants sheets with detents and a grabber, with Cancel on the leading side and Done on the trailing side (the button placement guidance was updated 24 March 2026) ([HIG: Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). Toolbars should have at most three groups, should never mix a text button and a symbol button in one group, and should have one prominent primary action on the trailing side ([HIG: Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)). Haptics should follow their documented meanings: notification for task outcomes, impact for snapping, selection for value changes. They should never be overused ([HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)). Motion should be purposeful, brief and optional under Reduce Motion ([HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)).

Two late-2026 developments shape the target. First, **users now choose their own glass opacity**. iOS 26.1 added a Clear/Tinted setting ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-26-1-lets-you-adjust-liquid-glass-transparency-on-your-iphone-heres-how-to-do-it)). WWDC26 says iOS 27 glass "automatically responds to the new Liquid Glass slider" ([WWDC26 session 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)). Custom glass must therefore stay legible across a range of opacities, which argues for system components and `.regular` glass over hand-tuned translucency. Second, **iPhone apps become resizable on iOS 27**, so layouts should key off size classes, not idiom or fixed frames ([WWDC26 269](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/269/)). That matters for homemesh's fixed `.frame(height: 320)` compare canvas (`homemesh/ios/HomemeshCapture/CompareView.swift:31`) and for jobtrack's fixed 220×400 columns (`jobtrack-ios/Jobtrack/BoardView.swift:16`). The iOS 27 visual refinements are known only from beta-era press: darker glass edges, a uniform top bar effect, and reportedly lighter dark-mode glass ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27/)). Apple's own iOS 27 release notes were not read. As of the 13 October 2026 runner image, GitHub's `macos-26` image ships Xcode 26.6 and no stable Xcode 27 ([runner-images macos-26-arm64](https://github.com/actions/runner-images/blob/main/images/macos/macos-26-arm64-Readme.md)). Building on the iOS 26 SDK is still the right baseline, and an Xcode 27 leg can follow once a stable image exists.

## The five changes, ranked by value to homemesh

The audit read the four apps at `file:line` precision but ran no simulator, so claims about truncation and dark-mode contrast below are inferred from point widths and colour literals, not observed. The effort figures are this report's estimates for one developer working in Swift, not measured numbers. Bare file names refer to homemesh's `ios/HomemeshCapture/`; parley paths are under parley's `ios/Parley/Views/` unless written in full. homemesh targets **iOS 17.0** (`homemesh/ios/project.yml:12-13`), so every iOS 26 API named below needs an `#available(iOS 26, *)` gate with a pre-26 fallback. parley, mailroom-ios and jobtrack-ios target iOS 26.0 and can use those APIs directly.

### 1. Rebuild the plan screen around a floating tool palette and a sheet inspector

The plan is homemesh's main work surface, and it has the app's only *functional* styling defects, which is why it ranks first. All eight `PlanTool` cases ("Select, Outlet, Jack, Access point, Internet, Measure, Stair, Zone", `PlanScreen.swift:3-6`) sit in one `.pickerStyle(.segmented)` (`PlanScreen.swift:45-47`). In zone mode a second picker appears with long labels such as "Where it must not" (`PlanScreen.swift:50-52`). On a 440 pt-wide iPhone 17 Pro Max that leaves roughly 50 pt per segment, so "Access point" almost certainly truncates (inferred). Selecting anything renders a full `Form` inside the same `VStack`, clamped to `maxHeight` 240–420 (`PlanScreen.swift:98-120`), and the canvas shrinks to whatever space remains. Save and Delete are repeated as form rows in four inspectors (`PlanScreen.swift:293-298,366-373,404-405,480-485`; `ZoneInspector.swift:41-42`). `PlanCanvas` declares `var zoom: CGFloat = 1` but nothing ever sets it (`PlanCanvas.swift:46`), so large floors stay at fit-to-screen scale. The canvas sits in a `Color(.secondarySystemBackground)` box (`PlanScreen.swift:72`) and draws `.black` marker strokes, a fixed `Color(white: 0.78)` grey and white diff cells (`PlanCanvas.swift:17,27,178-190`). In dark mode those strokes vanish and the white cells glare (inferred). Furniture labels use a fixed 9 pt font (`PlanCanvas.swift:116`).

The change has four parts. First, move the tools into a bottom-bar `ToolbarItemGroup` of SF Symbol buttons, each with an accessibility label. Keep Select, Measure and Undo on the top level, and put the four marker tools (Outlet, Jack, Access point, Internet) plus Stair and Zone behind `Menu`s, with the zone sub-mode as a picker inside its menu. That follows the HIG rules of at most three groups and moving secondary actions into a menu when the bar "is feeling too crowded" ([HIG: Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars); [WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). The bar floats as glass automatically on iOS 26 and stays a standard bottom bar on iOS 17. Split the groups with `ToolbarSpacer` (iOS 26.0, gated) ([Apple: ToolbarSpacer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/toolbarspacer)). Second, move the inspector into a `.sheet` with `presentationDetents([.height(…), .medium, .large])` (iOS 16) and a drag indicator, which the HIG recommends for progressive disclosure ([HIG: Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets); [Apple: presentationDetents](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/presentationdetents(_:))). The furniture sheet already does this at `.medium` (`PlanScreen.swift:523-527`). `.inspector` (iOS 17) is the alternative if iPad matters ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)). Save then becomes a single trailing confirm action (`ButtonRole.confirm` on iOS 26, [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/buttonrole/confirm); `.borderedProminent` before). Third, wire the dormant `zoom` to a pinch gesture plus pan. Fourth, let the canvas run full-bleed under the bars instead of in a grey box, and move its colours onto tokens that have dark variants. The result is a plan that keeps the whole screen, tools that never truncate, and an inspector that peeks over the plan instead of crushing it. This pattern is specific to homemesh. Its nearest relative elsewhere is jobtrack's future card-detail sheet.

### 2. Replace footnote status with a feedback layer people can see and feel

Every homemesh screen reports outcomes through a `@State` string shown as footnote text: **52 assignments** such as "Saved." and "Could not save: …" (`AppModel.swift:37-44`; `PlanScreen.swift:84,236-239`). The status line can sit off-screen. A processed room only sets `status = "Room added: name it below."` (`ScanScreen.swift:193-199`). An upload advances through three text strings (`ScanScreen.swift:264-292`) on a `URLSession.shared.upload(for:from:)` call that reports no progress (`Uploader.swift:46`). The plan job polls a list of text lines every 1.5 s (`PlanRun.swift:128,193-199`). Loading is a bare, unlabelled `ProgressView()` in five views. The empty house list is the string "No houses yet. Tap + to create one." (`AppModel.swift:42`). **None of the four apps contains a single haptic call, and homemesh has no animation at all** (greps for `sensoryFeedback`, `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator`, `withAnimation`, `.transition(` and `symbolEffect` find nothing).

Apple supplies every piece needed. `ContentUnavailableView` (iOS 17) is the recommended view for "a network error, a list without items, a search that returns no results" ([Apple: ContentUnavailableView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/contentunavailableview)). Use it for no houses, no rooms on a floor, and "RoomPlan found no furniture". `sensoryFeedback(_:trigger:)` (iOS 17) plays system haptics when a value changes ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/sensoryfeedback(_:trigger:))). Map it to the HIG categories: `.success` when a room is processed, a plan is saved, an upload finishes or a placement is confirmed; `.error` or `.warning` on failure; `.selection` when the tool changes. The HIG asks that haptics complement visual feedback and never stand alone ([HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)). "Saved." becomes a transient toast with Undo, wired to the revision-based Undo homemesh already has (`PlanScreen.swift:87-93,219-230`). Errors become an inline banner with a tone symbol, not red or orange text. Loading becomes `ProgressView("Loading plan…")`. The upload becomes determinate, which requires switching from `upload(for:from:)` to a progress-reporting upload task. A completed scan can draw a checkmark with `symbolEffect` (iOS 17) or `DrawOnSymbolEffect` (iOS 26) ([Apple: symbolEffect](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/symboleffect(_:options:value:)); [Apple: DrawOnSymbolEffect](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/symbols/drawonsymboleffect)). Animate state changes with `Animation.smooth` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/smooth)), and fall back to fades under `accessibilityReduceMotion` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityreducemotion)).

This change ranks second because it touches every screen, and it is the clearest case for a shared library. In parley, errors are red footnote text (`parley/ios/Parley/Views/ConversationView.swift:59-64`; `ReaderPlayerView.swift:80`; `SettingsView.swift:46`). parley's empty conversation is plain text even though its list uses `ContentUnavailableView` (`ConversationView.swift:47-52` vs `ConversationsView.swift:43`). parley also gives no haptic on send, on dictation start or stop, or when a reply is ready, which matters most because the Action Button starts dictation. mailroom's design requires that "Every action shows an Undo toast for the hold" (`mailroom/docs/design-inbox-zero.md:505-506`) and needs an inbox-zero empty state. jobtrack needs empty columns and a drop haptic.

### 3. Turn the house screen from a wall of links into a hub

After two good prominent buttons, `HouseView` lists its features as **seven plain-text `NavigationLink`s**: "Floors: order and ground level", "Add a floor", "3D view of the house", "Calibrate to my measurements", "Plan a mesh", "Equipment to plan with" and "Accuracy: scan against tape" (`HouseView.swift:95-103`). They have no icons, no grouping and no hierarchy. Each floor section opens with another text link (`HouseView.swift:80`). Room rows are three stacked lines with a coloured status word (`HouseView.swift:139-150`). The upload queue row offers two borderless text buttons with no swipe, progress or error state (`HouseView.swift:65-75`). Creating a house, floor or basement happens in an `.alert` with a `TextField` (`HousesView.swift:30-34`; `HouseView.swift:118-122`; `FloorsView.swift:71-75`). A validation failure lands in a footnote at the top of the list instead of beside the field (`AppModel.swift:51,63`). `FloorsView` pins edit mode on permanently (`FloorsView.swift:68`).

Rebuild the screen as sections of `Label(title, systemImage:)` rows. Put "Plan a mesh" in a primary section next to the two scan buttons, since it is the app's purpose. Group 3D, Calibrate, Accuracy and Equipment under a tools section, and floors under their own. Give room rows a title, a secondary line and a status chip that pairs a symbol and a word with its tone, because the HIG forbids colour as the only signal ([HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Upload rows get swipe actions and inline progress. Creation moves into a small sheet at a `.height` detent with inline validation, Cancel on the leading side and Done on the trailing side ([HIG: Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). On iOS 26 those buttons use `ButtonRole.close` and `.confirm` ([Apple: close](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/buttonrole/close)). Floors gets a standard `EditButton` instead of a pinned edit mode. On iOS 26, `navigationSubtitle` can carry a summary such as "3 floors · 2 rooms not placed" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/navigationsubtitle(_:))). This is cheap, highly visible work, and it ranks below the feedback layer only because it covers one screen family. The same row-and-chip pattern is what mailroom's design already specifies: inboxes with counts and endpoint health, rows with a bucket chip and an unread dot, and swipe actions (`mailroom/docs/design-inbox-zero.md:487-512`). It would also tidy parley's `ConversationRow`.

### 4. Give every app a brand and one meaning per colour

homemesh, mailroom-ios and jobtrack-ios have **no asset catalog at all**. They ship the blank default icon and system blue. parley has an icon, but as iOS 18-style PNG variants, not a layered Icon Composer icon (`parley/ios/Parley/Assets.xcassets`). iOS 26 icons are layered and respond to light, with default, dark, clear and tinted variants. They should avoid text and photos and "let the system handle blurring and other visual effects" ([HIG: App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons), updated 28 October 2026). Meanwhile homemesh's orange means at least eight things: "not on the plan yet", "guess", "staircase needs input", "misses a must-have", demand zones, movable furniture and door openings (`HouseView.swift:49,147`; `PlanScreen.swift:402`; `PlanRun.swift:220`; `PlanCanvas.swift:113,131,136`). Green means "on the plan", "ground floor", "auto placement", "jack" and "allow". The only badge is hand-rolled (`PlanRun.swift:228-230`).

The fix has three parts. Add an Icon Composer icon per app. Set the accent to scshafe-ui's accent, `#0a4f99` in light mode and `#6cb6ff` in dark (`scshafe-ui/tokens.css` at origin/main), so the iOS apps visibly belong to the same family as the web and desktop apps. Adopt the five tones that scshafe-ui and scshafe-qt already share (neutral, info, ok, warn and danger, plus buckets 1–8 on desktop), each with a fixed symbol. Map homemesh's statuses onto them by meaning. "On the plan" becomes ok. "Needs input" becomes warn. "Invalid" becomes danger. Plan-canvas categories such as zones and furniture use bucket colours, never status tones. Two cautions apply. Apple asks that custom colours ship "light and dark variants, and an increased contrast option for each variant" ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)). scshafe-ui's AA contrast tests were run against its own backgrounds (`#f6f8fb` and `#08111f`), not iOS system backgrounds, so contrast must be re-checked on device. And per Apple, the accent belongs on primary actions and in content, not smeared across glass ([HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). This change ranks fourth on value, but it is the cheapest of the five and unblocks the others. It should be *done* first.

### 5. Shrink glass to the controls that float over the camera

homemesh's only custom glass, `GlassBackground`, is correct in mechanism: `glassEffect` on iOS 26, `.ultraThinMaterial` before (`ScanScreen.swift:303-317`). It is wrong in scope. One large glass card holds a tip, a summary, a floor picker, the reason text, story pickers, staircase pickers, the status, Discard and the main button, all in footnote text, inside a 280 pt scroll (`ScanScreen.swift:71-126,109`). That breaks Apple's "sparingly" rule and puts text-heavy content on glass. The primary button cycles through four titles with no symbol (`ScanScreen.swift:117`), and Save is a plain glass capsule, not a prominent action (`ScanScreen.swift:136-139`). The fix keeps glass only on the floating controls: back, Save as `.buttonStyle(.glassProminent)` (iOS 26; `.borderedProminent` before), and a Start/Done button with a symbol. Group them in a `GlassEffectContainer` (iOS 26), as Apple recommends for rendering and morphing ([Apple: Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views); [Apple: glassProminent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/primitivebuttonstyle/glassprominent)). Move the floor, story and staircase choices into a pre-scan step or a detented sheet. The live camera is the one media-rich backdrop where `.clear` glass could be justified, but only with a 35% dimming layer ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). Since users can now push glass toward Clear themselves, `.regular` is the safer default.

The larger beneficiary is parley. Its composer, listening bar and reader controls each paint a `.background(.bar)` strip over scrolling content (`ConversationView.swift:129,206`; `ReaderPlayerView.swift:123`). That is exactly the "custom backgrounds... might overlay or interfere with... the scroll edge effect" problem ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)). Delete the strips and host the composer in `safeAreaBar` (iOS 26), which extends the scroll edge effect where `safeAreaInset` does not ([Apple: safeAreaBar](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/safeareabar(edge:alignment:spacing:content:))). Replace the `.roundedBorder` field (`ConversationView.swift:192`) with a glass capsule and a `.glassProminent` send button. Size the fixed 54 pt play button (`ReaderPlayerView.swift:105`) with a text style or a scaled metric. mailroom-ios and jobtrack-ios should each allow at most one floating glass action, compose or add-card. Their rows and cards stay in the content layer.

## scshafe-ios should copy scshafe-qt's pipeline, from a fresh token snapshot

The shared-library question is mostly answered by precedent the owner already has. scshafe-ui's typed token registry is the single source of truth. scshafe-qt proves the native pattern. `tools/gen_tokens.py --refresh` reads `@scshafe/ui`'s registry and writes a committed `tokens/sui-tokens.json` snapshot (schema 1, recording source version, file sha256, commit and a registry hash). It then generates `SuiTheme.qml`, and `--check` fails CI when either file is stale (`scshafe-qt/tools/gen_tokens.py:1-21`; scshafe-qt README "Token pipeline"). scshafe-ios should consume the *same* snapshot schema with a Swift emitter rather than introduce Style Dictionary. Style Dictionary's predefined `ios-swift` transform groups emit UIKit `UIColor`, not SwiftUI `Color` ([Style Dictionary: transform groups](https://styledictionary.com/reference/hooks/transform-groups/predefined/)). DTCG 2025.10 is a stable Candidate Recommendation, but it pushes light/dark modes into a separate resolver module ([DTCG Format 2025.10](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/format/)). Neither buys anything for three in-house consumers.

**Drift is already the main risk.** The owner's local scshafe-ui checkout sits at **0.5.0** (f8f98cc), while `origin/main` is at **0.9.0** (21 October 2026). The 0.9.0 registry has **77 tokens** and a `material` category. It brings in the dark-glass look (0.6.0) and the dark-theme AA fix (0.8.0). scshafe-qt's committed snapshot is still from **0.4.1**, with 62 tokens and no glass, `radius-xl`, `radius-pill` or easing tokens. The difference is concrete. The stale local file gives radii of 6/8/10 px, while 0.9.0 gives `radius-sm` 8, `radius-md` 12, `radius-lg` 16, `radius-xl` 20 and `radius-pill` 999. 0.9.0 also adds glass tokens (`--sui-glass: rgba(24,36,56,0.82)` in dark, `glass-accent-text #c4e2ff`) and motion tokens (`duration` 120 ms, `ease` `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.8,0.2,1)`, `ease-spring` `cubic-bezier(0.34,1.36,0.64,1)`). Generating from the local checkout would ship last month's design system. The first step is therefore to fetch scshafe-ui and generate from 0.9.0 (origin, or the published package). scshafe-qt's snapshot should be refreshed in the same pass, and bumping the token source should become a checklist item in all three repositories. The cleaner long-term fix is for scshafe-ui to publish the snapshot JSON itself as a release artefact, so that Qt and iOS read one file instead of each running its own reader.

### Package structure: two products, one glass shim, iOS 17 floor

The proposed layout is one Swift package. It declares `platforms: [.iOS(.v17)]`, with `.macOS(.v14)` optional for host-side previews, and `swift-tools-version: 6.0` in Swift 5 language mode. That mode has to build cleanly both under homemesh's Swift 5.9 with complete strict concurrency (`homemesh/ios/project.yml:19-22`) and under the other apps' Swift 5.0 setting. Which language mode is best was not verified. The package ships two products. **`ScshafeTokens`** is generated and contains no views, only `Color`, `CGFloat`, `Animation` and type-role values, so a widget or watch extension can take tokens alone. **`ScshafeUI`** holds styles, modifiers and components. A `Catalog/` XcodeGen app, matching how the apps are built, depends on the package by local path. Like scshafe-qt's gallery and Orbit's Storybook, it stays out of consumers' graphs ([kiwicom/orbit-swiftui](https://github.com/kiwicom/orbit-swiftui)). Orange's OUDS, the best maintained public SwiftUI design system, uses the same product split and injects its theme through the environment ([Orange-OpenSource/ouds-ios](https://github.com/orange-opensource/ouds-ios)). Resources, if any, go through `Bundle.module` ([Apple: Bundling resources](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/bundling-resources-with-a-swift-package)). Fonts stay system (SF Pro and SF Mono), which avoids package font registration entirely.

The generator maps the snapshot as follows. Names convert to camelCase, as in Qt (`--sui-text-strong` becomes `textStrong`). px converts to pt 1:1, and `rgba()` to sRGB `Color`. Spacing, radius and motion become `SuiSpace`, `SuiRadius` and `SuiMotion` namespaces, with the easing curves as `Animation.timingCurve`. Themed colours resolve light, dark and increased contrast. Apple advises putting colour and contrast variants in an asset catalog rather than branching on `colorSchemeContrast` in code ([Apple: colorSchemeContrast](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/colorschemecontrast)). That favours emitting a package `.xcassets`, at the cost of less diffable output than generated `UIColor(dynamicProvider:)` code. The owner should pick one. `glass-blur`, `glass-saturate` and the `clamp-*` layout tokens are dropped or kept for the pre-26 fallback only, because the OS renders real glass. scshafe-ui has no type scale, so iOS maps text roles to Dynamic Type styles in a native-only section, just as Qt adds its own type scale, buckets 1–8 and tone helpers (`toneFill` at 12%, `toneBorder` at 45%). iOS should reuse Qt's bucket values verbatim so that mailroom's bucket chips match the desktop app. A theme `@Entry` on `EnvironmentValues` (the macro is listed back to iOS 13 ([Apple: Entry()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/entry()))) carries app overrides and a `suiTransparency` value, the iOS analogue of scshafe-ui's `data-sui-transparency`.

Liquid Glass enters through exactly one modifier, seeded from homemesh's `GlassBackground`. **`.suiGlass(_:in:)`** calls `glassEffect(_:in:)` on iOS 26, which is declared with only a `Glass` and a `Shape`. Some community pages show a non-existent `intensity:` parameter, which is wrong ([Apple: glassEffect](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffect(_:in:))). Before iOS 26 it falls back to `.ultraThinMaterial` with the `glass-edge` stroke. Both branches go solid on the `popover` token under Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast or a forced `suiTransparency`, mirroring scshafe-ui 0.6.0. System glass already adapts to these settings on its own: Reduce Transparency makes it "frostier", and Increase Contrast adds borders ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). The custom fallback has to do this itself. `SuiGlassContainer` wraps `GlassEffectContainer` on 26 and passes through before. Button styles return the system `.glass` and `.glassProminent` on 26 and a custom `ButtonStyle` before. Following scshafe-ui's doctrine, the library applies glass only to the functional layer and keeps content (cards, rows, messages) on solid `card` and `panel` tokens. Components follow SwiftUI's style protocols (`ButtonStyle`, `LabelStyle`, `LabeledContentStyle`) rather than wrapper views, which would lose label customisation and future OS behaviour ([Swift with Majid](https://swiftwithmajid.com/2020/02/19/mastering-buttons-in-swiftui/)). They keep scshafe-qt's parity names and rules: `SuiChip`, `SuiBanner`, `SuiEmptyState` and `SuiToast`; an icon button that *requires* an accessible label; tones that always carry a symbol and a hidden prefix; and no colour literals outside the generated file, enforced by a grep test like Qt's `tests/test_rules.py`.

### Testing, CI and releases

CI splits by cost. The token `--check` runs on `ubuntu-latest` against the committed snapshot, just as Qt's does. Build, unit and snapshot tests run on `macos-26` with Xcode 26.6 pinned through `xcode-select`, matching homemesh's and mailroom-ios's existing workflows. The image provides iOS 26.2, 26.4 and 26.5 simulator runtimes and no iOS 17 or 18 runtime ([runner-images macos-26-arm64](https://github.com/actions/runner-images/blob/main/images/macos/macos-26-arm64-Readme.md)). Snapshot tests use pointfreeco's swift-snapshot-testing. Each component state is rendered across light and dark, default and an accessibility text size, and standard and increased contrast, plus one pass with `suiTransparency` forced to reduce. Snapshots run on one pinned simulator (for example iPhone 17 on iOS 26.5), because "Snapshots must be compared using the exact same simulator" that recorded them ([pointfreeco/swift-snapshot-testing](https://github.com/pointfreeco/swift-snapshot-testing)). Covering the pre-26 fallback needs a downloaded iOS 18 runtime or a separate job, at a cost this research did not measure. Glass snapshots should be few, because the iOS 27 SDK changes how glass renders and they will churn. The Catalog app builds in CI so it cannot rot, and a DocC catalog ("Getting started", "Tokens", "Glass and fallbacks", "Accessibility contract") can be published to GitHub Pages as Orbit did. Releases copy scshafe-ui's contract: an annotated `v<x.y.z>` tag on `main`, never deleted, replaced or reused. Each CHANGELOG entry and the generated file header record the scshafe-ui version the tokens came from. While the library is 0.x, apps pin it through XcodeGen `packages:` with up-to-next-minor, since a 0.x minor may break.

**API names the notes could not verify against Apple pages:** `MagnifyGesture`, the swipe-action modifiers, `contentTransition(.symbolEffect…)`, `Image(systemName:variableValue:)`, `LabeledContentStyle`, `@ScaledMetric`, the `ShapeStyle where Self == Color` extension syntax, the `@Animatable` macro (only a secondary source), the exact OS version of the slider tick API, and the snapshot trait names. Also unverified: whether `ControlSize.extraLarge` renders differently on iPhone (Apple's doc still says it resolves to `.large` outside visionOS ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/controlsize/extralarge))), the current swift-snapshot-testing version, and whether SPM accepts `v`-prefixed tags. The audit also flagged `GlassEffectContainer`, `.buttonStyle(.glass)`, `tabBarMinimizeBehavior` and `sensoryFeedback` as prior knowledge, but the design-language research confirmed all four against Apple's pages with the minimum OS versions given above. Single-source or press-only claims also need checking: that a `Menu` inside a `GlassEffectContainer` broke the morph on iOS 26.0–26.1 ([Medium](https://medium.com/@veersr9/ios-26-liquid-glass-in-swiftui-a-practical-reference-demo-app-liquidglassreference-69b8d46dc055)), that the App Store will require an Xcode 27 baseline around April 2027 ([lacquer #517](https://github.com/patrickserrano/lacquer/issues/517)), and whether SceneKit, used by homemesh's 3D view, is deprecated.

## v0.1 ships the feedback kit to homemesh and parley

scshafe-ui's extraction rule, "two real consumers, byte-identical render, no reverse-imports" (`scshafe-ui/docs/FRONTEND-DOCTRINE.md:40-41`), sets the scope. **scshafe-ios 0.1.0** should contain only pieces that homemesh and parley both need today. The first is `ScshafeTokens`, generated from the 0.9.0 snapshot with colours, space, radius and motion. Next come `SuiTone` with Qt's tone helpers and buckets, the `.suiGlass` shim, `SuiGlassContainer` and `.sui` button styles. The components are `SuiChip`, replacing homemesh's `badge(_:_:)` and parley's `TurnStateBadge`; `SuiBanner`, replacing both apps' footnote and red-text errors; `SuiEmptyState`, a thin layer over `ContentUnavailableView`; `SuiToast` with Undo, which mailroom also requires; a `Loadable` wrapper for homemesh's five repeated loading patterns; and `SuiHaptics`, which encodes the success, warning and selection conventions over `sensoryFeedback`. The Catalog app, the snapshot suite and the token check ship with it. homemesh adopts first, because it is the main app, gains the most and exercises the iOS 17 fallback. parley adopts second, before the 0.1.0 tag, so every component meets the two-consumer bar. mailroom-ios and jobtrack-ios start on 0.1 as greenfield consumers. Pocket ID sign-in is the strongest duplication candidate. It is written twice already (parley's 441-line `ParleyKit/OIDC.swift` with `TokenSession` and `Keychain`, and homemesh's 163-line `Auth.swift`), and mailroom plans to copy "the same pattern". It belongs in a separate `ScshafeAuth` product in v0.2, after a line-by-line diff that this research did not do. Security-sensitive logic should not ride along with a UI kit's first release.

| Decision | Options | Lean |
|---|---|---|
| Name | `scshafe-ios` (parallels `scshafe-qt`) or `scshafe-swiftui`; Swift types prefixed `Sui` for parity with `SuiTheme` | `scshafe-ios`, `Sui` prefix |
| Visibility | Public, or private | Public: GitHub Actions does not run for private repositories on this account, and the Catalog, snapshot and build CI needs macOS runners. scshafe-ui and scshafe-qt are already public. Private means verifying locally on the owner's Mac. |
| First adopting app | homemesh (iOS 17, biggest gain) or parley (iOS 26, cleanest code) | homemesh first, parley before the tag |
| homemesh floor | Keep iOS 17, or raise to 26 like the other three apps | Owner's call. Raising to 26 deletes every fallback branch and the iOS 17 snapshot job. |
| Appearance | Follow system light/dark, or pin dark glass as some web apps do | Follow system. The docs do not state the owner's preference. |
| Colour delivery | Generated asset catalog (Apple's contrast-variant path) or generated code | Owner's call. Either way, regenerate from 0.9.0 and refresh Qt's 0.4.1 snapshot. |
| Token source | Per-repo reader, or scshafe-ui publishes the snapshot | scshafe-ui publishes, to end the drift |

## Conclusion

The research reframes "make it sleeker" in two ways. On iOS 26 and 27 polish is subtractive and semantic. Apple's system already renders the expensive part, the glass. The work left to the developer is to stop painting over it, to give each colour one meaning, and to make outcomes felt rather than written in footnote text. homemesh's problems are almost all of that kind. Its accessibility work is already better than most apps': canvas VoiceOver summaries, labelled pickers and hints. Its plain, instructive wording is good. What it lacks is presentation, which is why roughly two weeks of focused changes, and not a redesign, would move it from engineering tool to native app.

The second reframing concerns the library. The hard part of scshafe-ios is not SwiftUI; it is keeping three platforms on one design system. The evidence from this audit is that drift has already happened twice. The local web checkout is four releases stale, and the Qt snapshot is five releases behind. An iOS library built carelessly would make it three. Making the token snapshot a published artefact of scshafe-ui, checked in CI by every consumer, matters more than any single component. Shipping v0.1 as the feedback kit lets that pipeline prove itself on the components every app needs before it has to carry anything as sensitive as sign-in.
